1) The Rewrites tab's step information sits flush against the card border and renders the explanation's backticks literally. 2) The Pipeline tab's stage chips wrap onto multiple lines with nothing signalling execution order. Both sections show the current state first, then the options.

2 · Pipeline: stage order across wrapped lines

The 8 chips (Global config, Inherited config, Parse, Migrate, Massage, Validate, Presets, Merge) are a wrapping flex row. On one line, left-to-right reads as order; the moment it wraps, nothing says whether reading continues on the next line or the lines are parallel groups. All variants below render in a narrow harness that forces the wrap.

Now — wrapped, no order signal

2A · Arrows between chips (recommended)

A muted → between consecutive chips. Because the arrow is its own flex item, a wrap puts it at the head of the continuation line — precisely where the "this continues from above" cue is needed. No chip geometry changes; the arrows are aria-hidden decoration.

2B · Ordinals inside the chips

A small muted number leads each chip. Order survives any wrap and is even sortable at a glance ("which stage is 5th?"), but it adds a permanent character to every chip and reads busier next to the dot + count that are already there.

2C · Joined segments (breadcrumb / stepper look)

Chips fuse into runs with shared borders — inside a line the sequence is unmistakable, but the wrap point still needs interpretation, the pill silhouette that the rest of the app uses disappears, and the selected-chip outline needs z-index games.

2D · Never wrap — one line, horizontal scroll

Geometry itself states the order, and arrows become unnecessary. But it hides stages off-screen at exactly the widths where the problem occurs — an error dot on a hidden stage is worse than an ambiguous wrap — and horizontal scrolling inside a column is easy to miss.

Recommendation: 2A. The arrows say "this is a sequence" without touching chip geometry, and the wrap behavior works in its favor: a continuation line leads with an arrow, which is the exact cue that was missing. All stages stay visible (unlike 2D), chips stay uncluttered (unlike 2B), and the app-wide pill silhouette survives (unlike 2C). If arrows alone ever prove insufficient at a glance, 2B's ordinals compose cleanly on top — but start with arrows only.
